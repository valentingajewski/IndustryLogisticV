Good Career vs Fun differences

Starting state.
Fun: start with a healthy profit balance, all industries active, and easy access to vehicles.
Career: start nearly broke, with only one small truck and a few reachable industries unlocked.

Vehicle access.
Fun: any configured vehicle can be spawned for free.
Career: vehicles have an upfront purchase cost, or at least a spawn fee / deposit / rental fee.
In-game effect: choosing a truck becomes a business decision instead of just convenience.

Upgrade pressure.
Fun: upgrades stay cheap and are mostly there for experimentation.
Career: upgrade costs matter and become the main long-term sink for profit.
In-game effect: the player chooses between buying capacity, production, or a better vehicle.

Cargo penalties.
Fun: cargo damage and loss are disabled or heavily softened.
Career: cargo condition directly reduces payout, and severe damage can void the job.
In-game effect: driving carefully matters.

Fuel and operating costs.
Fun: no fuel cost or only cosmetic fuel.
Career: each delivery subtracts fuel and maintenance from revenue.
In-game effect: long routes and heavy vehicles have tradeoffs.

Industry progression.
Fun: all industries can operate from the start.
Career: some industries require a minimum company level, a capital threshold, or a previous-node supply contract before they become worthwhile.
In-game effect: the map opens in stages.

Delivery profitability.
Fun: prices are stable and generous.
Career: prices fluctuate more, and low-demand deliveries can become barely profitable.
In-game effect: the player watches the network and picks smarter routes.

Failure state.
Fun: no real punishment beyond wasted time.
Career: bankruptcy risk, repossession of spawned vehicles, or temporary loss of access to upgrades if profit drops below zero.
In-game effect: mistakes matter without making the game unfair.

Time pressure.
Fun: production and loading are fast.
Career: normal production rates, normal transfer times, and maybe demand windows for some consumers.
In-game effect: planning the next stop matters.

Persistence consequences.
Fun: persistence can be optional and forgiving.
Career: industry state, cargo state, and money should always persist.
In-game effect: the player is building an ongoing business, not just a session toy.

Ideas that fit your current systems well

Career delivery contracts.
Instead of freeform hauling, stores or industries offer simple contracts like deliver 12t fuel to this station for a bonus.
This uses your existing load/unload flow but gives the player short-term goals.

Company level / reputation.
Gain reputation from successful deliveries and lose some from failed or damaged cargo.
Use reputation to unlock better trucks, new industries, and lower vehicle spawn fees.

Vehicle ownership tiers.
Fun can keep the current spawn menu.
Career can mark vehicles as:
Starter fleet
Rentable
Purchasable
Premium unlock
That gives the office menu a real progression role.

Soft difficulty bundles.
Instead of one abstract mode switch, Career can automatically enable:
fuel
cargo damage
industry persistence
spawn cost
upgrade cost scaling
Fun can automatically disable most of that.

Industry startup costs.
In Career, a newly discovered industry could require an activation payment before it joins your network.
That makes expansion feel intentional.

Warehouse or depot role.
Career could add one central depot that acts as a buffer for overflow cargo.
Fun would not need it.
This gives late-game players a logistics hub instead of only direct point-to-point hauling.

Debt and leasing.
If the player lacks cash, let them lease a vehicle with a daily payment or profit percentage cut.
That is more interesting than just blocking access.

Low-risk implementation plan

Make Fun and Career only change constants first.
Fun: free vehicle spawn, cheap upgrades, no penalties.
Career: spawn fees, higher upgrade costs, damage/fuel enabled, lower starting profit.
This gives immediate differentiation with little UI work.

Add one progression mechanic next.
Best option: vehicle purchase or rental cost.
That creates a money loop quickly.

Add one strategic mechanic after that.
Best option: contracts or industry unlock thresholds.
That gives Career a reason to exist beyond “harder numbers.”

If you want the cleanest identity
Use this rule:
Fun = sandbox logistics.
Career = trucking company simulation.

That framing keeps design decisions consistent.

A compact version you could ship first:

Fun: high starting cash, free vehicle spawn, cheap upgrades, no fuel, no cargo damage.
Career: low starting cash, vehicle spawn fee, upgrade costs unchanged or higher, fuel and cargo damage enabled, persistence always on.
Career phase 2: add contracts and unlockable vehicles.
